Pelumi's story

My journey with Mr Steve. How encouragement, practical support and faith helped Pelumi pursue dance and see what was possible.

Name
Ijelu Oluwapelumi Uswat
Age
21
Studied
Human Kinetics & Health Education, LASU
Today
Model, content creator & business owner
Mentor
Mr Steve

My journey with Mr Steve began during my teenage years at Elevation Church, when I was around 15 or 16. I don’t remember the exact moment we first met, but I remember how we gradually became closer through our involvement in the creative space. He was a church leader who had an interest in the creative arts, and was a rap master himself, while I was part of the creative team and eventually became the team lead for dance in the teenage church. We worked together, had conversations, and built a relationship that went beyond our roles. He became someone I could easily talk to, someone whose optimism about life and approach to opportunities left a lasting impression on me.

One of the things I appreciate most about Mr Steve is how he helped me see possibilities. He would share stories about his own journey, including his experiences pursuing rap, moving between places, and eventually finding his way to Lagos. He also shared lessons from the experiences of people around him, particularly about opportunities that can change the direction of a person’s life. I remember his story about how he missed an investment opportunity because the money wasn’t available at the time. The lesson was simple but profound: don’t always wait until everything is perfect before taking action. If an opportunity matters to you, explore the possibilities, find a way to prepare, and take the step you can. Those conversations shaped how I think about life and the future.

His support became especially meaningful when I wanted to pursue dance outside church. I was young, scared, and uncertain, but he encouraged me, reassured me, and helped me navigate the process. He volunteered to be my guardian, accompanied me, and supported me with transportation. He gave me my first tablet at a time when I didn’t have a phone and needed a way to access the internet. These may seem like different acts of kindness, but to me, they all communicated something important: I could pursue the things I wanted to do, and I didn’t have to figure everything out alone. His involvement made me take my aspirations more seriously and gave me the reassurance to keep going, even when I had doubts.

Mr Steve also introduced me to the world of investing. Through our conversations and calls, he explained concepts I had never encountered before, including what an IPO meant and how companies enter the market. He didn’t just explain these things; he encouraged me to learn, read, and understand them for myself. I certainly don’t know everything about investing today, but those conversations opened my mind to a world I might not otherwise have explored. He also saw possibilities in my business, Everything_Prettty, even when I was still trying to understand how to grow it. He would talk about distribution and other opportunities that I couldn’t fully grasp at the time. As I’ve grown, some of those ideas have started to make more sense, and I find myself looking back and understanding what he was trying to show me.

His influence also extended to how I approached leadership and personal responsibility. During my teenage years, I took my leadership role seriously. I felt that if I had been entrusted with a responsibility, I needed to make the most of it. Looking back, I believe those early experiences helped establish a foundation that prepared me for other lessons and influences I encountered later in life. Even when I make decisions today, I often find myself thinking beyond my immediate circumstances and considering what those decisions could mean for my future. I may not always consciously think, “Mr Steve taught me this,” but I can recognise the influence of those formative years in the way I approach opportunities. Above all, the importance of keeping God at the centre of my decisions has remained part of that foundation.

Today, I am a graduate of Lagos State University, where I studied Human Kinetics and Health Education. I am also a model, a content creator, and a business owner, and I have had opportunities to work with fashion and footwear brands, as well as a business-focused app. I still have a strong interest in dance and intend to return to dance school now that I have completed my degree. Looking ahead, I hope to pursue further studies in Human Kinetics and explore how movement, health, dance, and modelling can work together. I envision creating a space where dancers and other people can better understand their bodies, movement, and physical wellbeing. I also hope to contribute to helping people of different ages maintain healthy, active lives. These are ambitions I am still developing, but I am excited about the possibilities ahead.

Mentorship is not always about one defining moment. Sometimes, it is about conversations, reassurance, practical support, and the example someone sets through their own life.

When I reflect on Mr Steve’s impact, I realise that mentorship is not always about one defining moment or a single piece of advice. Sometimes, it is about the accumulation of conversations, reassurance, practical support, and the example someone sets through their own life. I admire his optimism, his willingness to pursue opportunities, and the way he works towards the things he believes in. Watching him continue to pursue his goals has reinforced the importance of being intentional about the future while taking action in the present. I am deeply grateful for the time I spent learning from him, and those memories are ones I will always cherish.

As I continue to grow, I hope to carry that same spirit of possibility into my own journey and eventually become someone who can encourage and support other young people. I would love to contribute to meaningful projects that create opportunities for young people to develop their talents, whether through dance, creativity, mentorship, or other initiatives. Mr Steve has been an important part of my story, and I hope that, in my own way, I can pass on some of the encouragement and support I received from him. I look forward to the possibility of working together on future projects, and I genuinely hope to see him again soon. I miss him, and I remain grateful for the role he has played in shaping the person I am becoming.
