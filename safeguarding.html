1.Our safeguarding commitment

Ohrus Global Youth Initiative is committed to creating a safe, inclusive, respectful and nurturing environment where children, young people, adults at risk, staff, volunteers and all participants can develop their gifts, discover their purpose and contribute meaningfully to society.

We recognise that every individual has inherent dignity and deserves to be treated with respect, compassion and fairness, irrespective of race, ethnicity, gender, disability, social background, faith or personal circumstances.

Our Christian foundation calls us to demonstrate love, integrity, service and accountability in every interaction. We therefore have zero tolerance for abuse, exploitation, neglect, bullying, harassment, discrimination, grooming or any form of behaviour that places another person at risk of harm.

Safeguarding is a collective responsibility shared by our trustees, leaders, staff, volunteers, parents, partners and participants.

2.Purpose of this policy

This policy establishes the principles, standards and procedures through which Ohrus Global Youth Initiative will:

  • Protect children and young people under 18 from abuse, neglect and exploitation.
  • Protect adults at risk of abuse or neglect.
  • Promote physical, emotional, psychological and spiritual well-being.
  • Ensure that all staff and volunteers understand their safeguarding responsibilities.
  • Provide clear procedures for reporting, recording and responding to concerns.
  • Ensure activities are appropriately supervised, risk-assessed and delivered in a safe environment.
  • Maintain accountability, transparency and compliance with applicable safeguarding legislation and guidance in England.

3.Scope

This policy applies to all trustees, directors, staff, volunteers, youth leaders, mentors, coaches, ministry leaders, creative arts facilitators, contractors and representatives acting on behalf of Ohrus Global Youth Initiative.

It covers all organisational activities, including:

  • Youth development and mentoring.
  • Music, singing and dance programmes.
  • Drama, acting and creative arts.
  • Sports and recreational activities.
  • Educational workshops and skills development.
  • Christian discipleship, prayer and pastoral support.
  • Community, medical and welfare outreaches.
  • Events, trips, transport and residential activities.
  • Online meetings, digital communications, photography and social media.

4.Safeguarding principles

Ohrus Global Youth Initiative will operate according to the following principles:

Prevention: We will identify risks and take reasonable steps to prevent harm before it occurs.

Protection: We will prioritise the safety and welfare of anyone at risk.

Participation: Children and young people will be encouraged to express their views, raise concerns and participate in decisions affecting them.

Accountability: Everyone will be expected to comply with safeguarding standards, and concerns will be addressed fairly and promptly.

Confidentiality: Safeguarding information will be handled securely and shared only with appropriate people who need it to protect individuals or fulfil legal duties.

Inclusion: No individual will be excluded from protection or treated unfairly because of their background, identity, disability or beliefs.

5.Types of abuse and harm

All representatives must be able to recognise and report possible indicators of:

  • Physical abuse: Hitting, assault, inappropriate restraint or other physical harm.
  • Emotional abuse: Threats, humiliation, intimidation, rejection or persistent degrading treatment.
  • Sexual abuse and exploitation: Sexual contact, inappropriate sexual behaviour, grooming, coercion or exploitation.
  • Neglect: Failure to meet essential care, supervision or safety needs.
  • Bullying and harassment: Including cyberbullying, discrimination and intimidation.
  • Financial or material abuse: Theft, coercion, exploitation or misuse of another person's resources.
  • Online harm: Inappropriate messaging, grooming, sharing harmful content or misuse of personal images.
  • Spiritual abuse: Using religious authority, scripture, prayer, prophecy, pastoral influence or threats of spiritual consequences to manipulate, control, silence or exploit another person.

This list is not exhaustive. Any behaviour that raises a reasonable concern about someone's safety or welfare must be taken seriously.

6.Safer recruitment and vetting

Ohrus Global Youth Initiative will take appropriate steps to establish the suitability of trustees, staff and volunteers before assigning responsibilities, particularly where roles involve children or adults at risk.

Depending on the role, these measures may include:

  • Application forms and identity verification.
  • References and relevant employment or volunteering history.
  • Safeguarding interviews and role-specific assessments.
  • Appropriate Disclosure and Barring Service (DBS) checks, including barred-list checks where legally eligible and required.
  • Safeguarding induction and training.
  • Ongoing supervision and review of conduct.

No individual will be permitted to undertake duties for which they are legally barred or otherwise unsuitable. A DBS check does not replace supervision, training or continuing safeguarding oversight.

7.Code of conduct

All representatives must:

  1. Treat every participant with dignity, respect and fairness.
  2. Maintain appropriate professional boundaries.
  3. Avoid being alone with a child in circumstances that are hidden, unauthorised or contrary to approved safeguarding arrangements.
  4. Use approved communication channels when contacting young participants and involve parents or carers where appropriate.
  5. Never engage in sexualised behaviour, grooming, bullying, intimidation, favouritism or exploitation.
  6. Never use physical punishment, humiliating discipline or inappropriate restraint.
  7. Obtain appropriate parental consent and participant assent for activities, trips, transport, photography and other relevant arrangements.
  8. Never promise absolute secrecy when a safeguarding concern is disclosed.
  9. Report concerns, allegations and breaches of this policy promptly.
  10. Cooperate with safeguarding investigations and lawful requests from relevant authorities.

8.Supervision, events and activities

Every activity involving children or adults at risk must have appropriate supervision and risk-management arrangements.

Before an activity, the responsible leader must consider:

  • The ages, needs and number of participants.
  • Suitable staffing and supervision arrangements.
  • Venue safety, accessibility and emergency procedures.
  • Attendance registers, emergency contacts and consent arrangements.
  • Safe collection, drop-off and transport procedures.
  • First aid, medical information and relevant dietary or accessibility needs.
  • Photography, filming and online safety.
  • Appropriate arrangements for changing areas, toilets and personal care.

Where practicable, activities involving children should be organised to avoid isolated one-to-one situations. Any necessary individual support must follow an agreed, risk-assessed and appropriately documented arrangement.

9.Photography, digital communication and online safety

Ohrus Global Youth Initiative will protect participants' privacy and dignity in all digital activities.

  • Appropriate consent must be obtained before taking or publishing identifiable images of children.
  • Images must not be used in ways that expose participants to embarrassment, exploitation or unnecessary risk.
  • Staff and volunteers must not request or share inappropriate images.
  • Personal messaging with children must follow approved communication procedures.
  • Online sessions must use suitable privacy and supervision arrangements.
  • Personal information, photographs and safeguarding records must be stored securely and handled in accordance with applicable data protection requirements.

Consent to photography or publicity must not be treated as consent to unrelated uses of personal information.

10.Pastoral care, prayer and counselling

Ohrus Global Youth Initiative recognises the importance of spiritual development, encouragement, prayer and pastoral support.

Spiritual care must never replace appropriate safeguarding action or professional assistance.

Pastoral leaders must not:

  • Pressure a participant to disclose private information publicly.
  • Use fear, condemnation, prophecy or religious authority to control a participant.
  • Discourage someone from reporting abuse or seeking medical, psychological or statutory support.
  • Promise that a disclosure will remain secret.
  • Conduct inappropriate counselling or physical contact.

Where a child or adult discloses abuse, the individual must be listened to calmly, reassured that speaking up was right, and referred through the appropriate safeguarding procedure.

11.Reporting a safeguarding concern

Any person who witnesses, suspects or receives a disclosure of abuse or neglect must act promptly. The reporting procedure is as follows:

  1. Ensure immediate safety. If someone is in immediate danger, call 999.
  2. Listen carefully. Allow the individual to speak without interruption. Do not ask leading questions or conduct your own investigation.
  3. Reassure the individual. Explain that they have done the right thing by speaking up, and that information may need to be shared with people who can help protect them.
  4. Record the concern. Write down the facts as soon as possible, including the date, time, observations and the person's own words where possible.
  5. Report immediately. Notify the Designated Safeguarding Lead or deputy as soon as possible.
  6. Refer appropriately. Where necessary, the safeguarding lead will contact the relevant local authority children's or adult safeguarding team, police or other statutory agency.
  7. Maintain records. Store information securely and document decisions, referrals and subsequent actions.

If the concern involves the Designated Safeguarding Lead, it must be reported to the Board of Trustees' nominated safeguarding trustee or directly to the appropriate statutory authority. If the concern involves a trustee, it must not be handled solely by the person implicated.

No internal process must delay a necessary referral to the police or local authority.

12.Responding to disclosures and allegations

Ohrus Global Youth Initiative will take all safeguarding concerns seriously, including concerns about trustees, leaders, staff, volunteers and external partners.

The organisation will not attempt to investigate allegations beyond what is necessary to establish immediate safety and make an appropriate referral. Decisions about further investigation belong to the relevant authorities.

Where an allegation concerns a staff member or volunteer, appropriate interim measures may be necessary to protect participants while a concern is assessed. Any action will be proportionate, fair and consistent with relevant procedures.

Retaliation against anyone who raises a safeguarding concern in good faith will not be tolerated.

13.Designated Safeguarding Lead

The Designated Safeguarding Lead will:

  • Receive and assess safeguarding concerns.
  • Maintain accurate and secure safeguarding records.
  • Coordinate appropriate referrals to statutory agencies.
  • Ensure staff and volunteers receive relevant training.
  • Support risk assessments and safer working practices.
  • Review incidents and recommend improvements.
  • Advise the trustees on safeguarding risks and compliance.

The Board of Trustees retains overall responsibility for ensuring the organisation has effective safeguarding arrangements.

14.Training and awareness

All trustees, staff and volunteers must receive safeguarding information appropriate to their responsibilities before undertaking relevant duties.

Those working directly with children or adults at risk must receive appropriate safeguarding training and periodic refresher training.

The organisation will ensure that safeguarding procedures, reporting routes and emergency contacts are communicated clearly and remain accessible.

15.Record-keeping and confidentiality

All safeguarding concerns, decisions, referrals and actions must be documented accurately, objectively and promptly.

Records must be stored securely, access must be restricted to authorised individuals, and information must be retained or disposed of in accordance with applicable law and the organisation's retention procedures.

Confidentiality must never be used as a reason to withhold information necessary to protect a child or adult at risk.

16.Policy monitoring and review

The Board of Trustees will review this policy at least annually, following a serious safeguarding incident, or whenever there are significant changes to relevant legislation, guidance or organisational activities.

All representatives are responsible for familiarising themselves with this policy and complying with its requirements.

17.Policy approval

This policy is subject to formal approval by the Board of Trustees of Ohrus Global Youth Initiative.
