Tomisin's story

A testimony of mentorship, growth and becoming. From a shy teenager to Senior Prefect, founder of Teens With a Difference, and a career in strategy and analytics.

Owolana Oluwatomisin Solomon
Age

Today
Strategy & Analytics

Mentor
Mr. Stephen Sedems

My name is Owolana Oluwatomisin Solomon, and I am 23 years old. My journey has been shaped by faith, growth, and a conscious commitment to becoming the man I believe I was created to be.

Mr. Stephen Sedems, fondly known to us as Bro Steph, was like a big brother to many of us years ago, back when we were just young boys who were still finding our feet and, honestly, not particularly serious about life. He would spend time with us, have meaningful conversations about life, and relate with us in a way that felt natural. He wasn't overly formal or unnecessarily strict; he was simply someone we enjoyed being around.

Beyond that, he was one of the men who consistently taught us about faith, responsibility, and leadership during our early teenage years in the church where I met him. Those lessons gradually shaped my worldview and instilled in me a deep sense of responsibility to make a difference wherever I find myself.

I grew from being a very shy young boy, struggled with low self-esteem, and lacked confidence in himself to becoming the Senior Prefect Boy of my secondary school. That transformation taught me to take responsibility, inspire others, and lead with purpose. It is a culture I have continued to uphold to this day.

In the very year I concluded my tenure as Senior Prefect Boy, at just 16 years old, I took that same culture back to the church. I began developing a vision for how young people could rise, take responsibility, and create positive change in their immediate environment. I gathered a team, brought Mr. Stephen into the vision, and, with his support and encouragement, we established a movement called Teens With a Difference.

Together, we built the structure, developed the system, invested in building capacity, and raised others who could carry the vision forward. Today, almost a decade later, the movement is still alive and running. To me, that is one of the clearest demonstrations of the enduring power of mentorship: when someone invests in you, you can grow to become someone who invests in others.

Today, I work in strategy and analytics at an investment firm in Nigeria while building my expertise in Data Engineering. It is encouraging to be part of a company that was recently featured in TIME AFRICA, and I remain committed to growing, contributing meaningfully, and becoming better at what I do.

One statement Mr. Stephen often made to us back then was, “We are going global.” Those words may have sounded simple at the time, but they stayed with me. As a young boy, hearing them expanded my imagination of what was possible and shaped my perspective on who I could become and what I could achieve.

Today, I find myself passing those same words and principles on to the younger children in my church, encouraging them to believe in their potential, take responsibility, and become agents of positive change in their generation.

Looking back, I am deeply grateful for the role Mr. Stephen Sedems played in my journey. His impact went beyond the lessons he taught; he helped shape the way I see myself, leadership, responsibility, and the possibilities that lie ahead.

Sometimes, the greatest impact a mentor can make is to help a young person see beyond who they are today and believe in who they can become.
