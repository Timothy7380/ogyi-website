Sharon's story

A foundation of faith, courage and purpose. How opportunities to serve as a teenager grew into a gift for speaking and hosting.

Name
Sharon Ejakpofo
Age
27
Studying
Business Administration (final year), National Open University of Nigeria
Gifts
Public speaking, compering & event hosting
Mentor
Uncle Steve

Some people make an impact on your life that you may not fully understand until you grow older. For me, Uncle Steve is one of those people.

He was one of the people who helped shape my Christian journey from my teenage years. Through him, I began to see Christianity differently. He showed me that being born again did not mean losing your joy or personality. I learnt that you could love God, live for Him, and still have fun. More importantly, he helped lead me to Christ and laid a foundation that continues to influence my life today.

Beyond my spiritual growth, Uncle Steve was instrumental in helping me develop courage and confidence. As teenagers in church, we were given opportunities to serve, volunteer, express ourselves, and take on responsibilities. Those opportunities may have seemed small at the time, but they were significant in shaping who I am today.

My journey into public speaking, compering, and hosting events began with the encouragement and opportunities I received during those formative years. I did not immediately understand the gifts God had placed within me, but as I grew, I began to recognise them. Looking back, I can see how the willingness of people like Uncle Steve to give young people a platform helped build the foundation for the skills and confidence I continue to develop today.

One of the most important lessons I learnt from him was that we can pursue and develop our gifts while remaining rooted in Christ. He also taught us the value of service and the understanding that serving God is never in vain. The reward for service may not always come immediately or in the way we expect, but there is value in faithfully giving ourselves to God's work.

I call him Uncle Steve because, to me, he has always been more than a Sunday school teacher or a brother in the church. He has been a mentor, a big brother, and someone whose influence has been part of my journey from my teenage years into adulthood.

Today, I am in my final year studying Business Administration at the National Open University of Nigeria. I also have over three years of administrative work experience and continue to grow in leadership, public speaking, event hosting, media, and volunteering. Each new opportunity reminds me of the importance of the foundations laid during my younger years.

As I look to the future, I hope to continue developing my gifts, growing in my career, and using the opportunities I receive to make a meaningful difference in the lives of others. I want to be someone who not only develops personally but also creates room for other people, especially young people, to discover their abilities, build courage, and step into their purpose.

Mentorship is about planting the right seeds, providing opportunities, offering encouragement, and helping a young person see what is possible.

My story reminds me that mentorship is not always about having all the answers or knowing exactly what someone will become. Sometimes, it is about planting the right seeds, providing opportunities, offering encouragement, and helping a young person see what is possible.

I am grateful that Uncle Steve was one of the people who planted those seeds in my life. As I continue to grow, I carry those lessons of faith, courage, service, and purpose with me.
